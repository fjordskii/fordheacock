How a follow-up runs

One lead becomes one row. A reply stops the chase. The automatic send is still off.

The path

  1. 1 · LeadSomeone fills the form, or another system posts to the same intake.
    2. ↓
  2. 2 · Already here?If that email is already queued, waiting on a reply, opted out, or bounced, no second row is added. You get a note. Otherwise a new row is queued, due today, on the first check-in template.
    3. ↓
  3. 3 · SendA manual run sends only rows that are queued and due. It skips paused, stopped, replied, bounced, and opted-out addresses. The row becomes “waiting on a reply.”
    4. ↓
  4. 4 · WatchThe inbox is checked for a message from that address, or in the same thread. The subject line does not matter.

What stops it

  1. They write backStatus becomes replied. Outcome is “replied,” unless a later outcome was already set. You are notified.
  2. They ask to stopWords like “remove me” or “unsubscribe” stop that address for good, including a later new row.
  3. The address bouncesThe row is marked bounced and is not emailed again. This is wired, and has not been seen on a real bounce yet.
  4. Three notes, no answerAfter three sends with no reply, the row stops and you are notified. A second and third template are not sent yet.

Not turned on

  1. Automatic sendThe 15-minute sender is off. Sends happen only when someone runs them.
  2. Their mailboxMail still goes out from your Gmail. Their address is only a setting.
  3. Their sheetThe queue still lives on your Drive. They do not own it yet.
  4. A real leadOnly test addresses have been through the loop.
Proven with a test inbox: one queued row, one send, one reply, outcome set to replied. No customer was emailed.
